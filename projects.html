Questions I’m Exploring

My research interests lie at the intersection of neuroscience, epigenetics, and neurodegenerative disease. I am particularly interested in understanding how changes in gene regulation influence neuronal function and contribute to Alzheimer’s disease. My work is driven by a broader curiosity about the molecular mechanisms that shape brain health, memory, and cognitive function.

DOCTORAL RESEARCH · 2025–PRESENT

Epigenetic Regulation in Alzheimer's Disease

University of Toronto · Zovkic Lab

Question

How do epigenetic mechanisms influence neuronal function, and how might changes in chromatin regulation contribute to Alzheimer's disease?

Approach

I use experimental and molecular neuroscience approaches to investigate how chromatin regulation influences cellular processes and cognitive function. My research integrates animal models, molecular analyses, and behavioural approaches to better understand the relationship between epigenetic regulation and neurodegenerative disease.

Why it matters

Understanding how epigenetic mechanisms contribute to neurodegeneration may provide new insights into the molecular processes underlying Alzheimer's disease and help identify potential avenues for future research.

RESEARCH FELLOWSHIP · 2025

Antidepressant Response in Postpartum Depression

Centre for Addiction and Mental Health · Galea Lab

Question

How are behavioural and cellular outcomes altered following antidepressant treatment in a rodent model of postpartum depression?

Approach

I examined behavioural outcomes alongside brain-region-specific molecular and cellular measures to investigate antidepressant response and neuroinflammatory signalling.

What I learned

This project introduced me to behavioural neuroscience and neurobiological models of disease and helped shape my transition from computational biology into experimental neuroscience.

HONOURS THESIS · 2024–2025

Biodiversity Persistence in Fragmented Landscapes

McMaster University · Kolasa Lab

Question

How can small habitat patches support greater biodiversity than larger patches when the total amount of habitat is held constant?

Approach

I developed agent-based computational models to investigate how habitat patch size, connectivity, dispersal, and species characteristics influence biodiversity persistence across fragmented landscapes.

What I learned

This project was my introduction to independent research and computational modelling. It shaped my interest in using quantitative approaches to investigate complex biological systems and gave me experience developing a research question, analytical framework, and computational pipeline independently.
